# Sourcing Candidates with Boolean

## Overview
Turn a job spec into search strings the recruiter can paste straight into LinkedIn, Google or a CV database. Good sourcing is about the words real candidates use on their profiles, not the words in the job ad.

## Before you start
If the recruiter has not given these, ask in one short message:
1. The job spec or role title
2. Location (and whether remote is fine)
3. Which tool they are searching: LinkedIn (free or Recruiter), Google, Indeed/CV database, or "not sure"
- If a step needs live web data you don't have (job ads, company news, salaries), say so and give the recruiter the exact search to run instead of guessing.

## Steps
1. **Pull out the must-haves.** List the 3–5 things a candidate cannot do without (title, core skill, licence, industry). Ignore nice-to-haves for now.
2. **Find the real-world words.** For each must-have, list the synonyms, older titles, abbreviations and spelling variants people put on profiles. Example: "Talent Acquisition Partner" also shows up as "Recruiter", "TA Specialist", "Recruitment Consultant", "Resourcer".
3. **Build three strings**, from narrow to wide:
   - **Tight:** title AND core skill (on LinkedIn, set location in the Locations filter, not the keywords)
   - **Medium:** title synonyms OR'd together AND one core skill
   - **Wide:** skills only, no title — finds people with odd job titles
4. **Adapt for the tool:**
   - LinkedIn: use the Title field for titles, keywords box for skills. Free LinkedIn caps search length, so keep each string short.
   - Google X-ray: start with `site:linkedin.com/in` and put the location in quotes. Google ignores NOT: use a minus sign with no space (`-intern -student`) and keep the string under 32 words.
   - CV databases: use their own Boolean box; most accept AND / OR / NOT and quotes.
5. **Add NOT terms** to drop the noise (e.g. NOT "student" NOT "intern" for senior roles, NOT "sales" when a title clashes).

## Output
Give the recruiter, in this order:
1. Must-haves (one line each)
2. The three strings in copy-ready code blocks, labelled Tight / Medium / Wide
3. One Google X-ray string
4. Two tips for this specific role (where these people hang out, what they call themselves). For trades and blue-collar roles, many people aren't on LinkedIn: also suggest local CV databases, Facebook trade groups and referrals.

## Quick reference
| Operator | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| AND | both words | recruiter AND mining |
| OR | either word | (fitter OR "fitter and turner") |
| NOT | leave out | NOT intern |
| " " | exact phrase | "talent acquisition" |
| ( ) | group | (HR OR "people ops") AND Cape Town |

## Common mistakes
- Copying job ad words. Ads say "rockstar"; profiles say "Senior Developer".
- One giant string. Three short ones beat one that LinkedIn cuts off.
- Forgetting OR needs brackets when mixed with AND.
- Lowercase "and/or" — most tools need capitals.
